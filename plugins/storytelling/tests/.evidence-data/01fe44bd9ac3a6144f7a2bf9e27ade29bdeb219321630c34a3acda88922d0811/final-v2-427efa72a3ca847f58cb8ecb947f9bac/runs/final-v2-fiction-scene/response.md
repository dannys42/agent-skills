The letter waited where Mara had left it, squarely in the table’s one clean patch.

Everything else in the room seemed determined to decay around it. Wallpaper peeled in damp tongues. Rain worried the loose windowpane, and each gust breathed soot from the cold grate across the floorboards. The house smelled of wet plaster and something sweetly rotten beneath it.

Mara kept her back rigidly to the table.

She tested the brass latch on the door. Locked. She straightened a crooked picture whose glass reflected a pale stranger with her mouth pressed thin. She counted the stains spreading across the ceiling: seven, if the long brown seam counted as one.

Behind her, paper whispered.

Only the draft, she told herself.

Her name was written on the envelope in a hand she had not seen for eleven years. She could picture every stroke without turning—the hooked M, the final a trailing off like a road.

Thunder pressed against the walls. Mara reached for the dead fireplace poker instead.

The letter remained unopened, but its first sentence had already begun inside her.
