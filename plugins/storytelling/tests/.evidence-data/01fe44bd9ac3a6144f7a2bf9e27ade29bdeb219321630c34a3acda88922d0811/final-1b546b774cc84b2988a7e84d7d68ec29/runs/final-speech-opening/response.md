You know the moment.

You’ve just left a meeting, and someone on your team has done something that can’t quite be ignored: a deadline slipped, a customer felt dismissed, a decision went sideways. You replay the conversation you need to have.

You want to be honest. You also don’t want to crush their confidence.

So you wait for the “right time.” You soften every sentence until the point disappears. Or you say it too quickly, with too little context, and spend the rest of the day wondering whether you helped at all.

If that sounds familiar, it’s not because you’re bad at feedback. It’s because feedback asks us to hold two things that can feel in conflict: “This needs to change,” and “I believe you can grow.”

Useful feedback is not a verdict on someone’s worth. It is a clear signal: here is what happened, here is why it matters, and here is the next step we can take.

Today, we’ll make that conversation less mysterious—so your people can leave it knowing where they stand, and believing they can move forward.
