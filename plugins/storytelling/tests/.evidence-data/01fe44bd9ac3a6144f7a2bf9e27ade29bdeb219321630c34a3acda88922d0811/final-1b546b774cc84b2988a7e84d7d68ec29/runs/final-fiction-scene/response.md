Mara kept her hands buried in the pockets of her coat, though the room was warm enough to sour the wool at her wrists. Rain ticked against the warped windowpanes. Each drop made the glass shiver in its lead frame, and the sound filled the pauses between the house’s small creaks.

On the table, beneath a brass lamp gone green at the base, lay the letter.

Its envelope was cream, its seal split cleanly by someone else’s thumb. Mara knew the handwriting without moving closer: her brother’s tight, slanted strokes, the way he wrote her name when he wanted something she could not give.

She crossed to the hearth instead. Ash feathered the grate. She nudged a blackened log with the poker until a red eye opened beneath it.

The letter waited behind her. She could feel it there, square and patient, as if it had learned the room’s old tricks: the damp smell, the sagging floorboards, the chill that gathered along the walls.

When the fire caught, Mara held out her palms. She did not turn around. Not yet.
